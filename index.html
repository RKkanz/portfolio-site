02 · Foundation Robotics · 2026

Teleoperation gloves

Lead engineer on the gloves operators use to drive a humanoid's hands: sensors, electronics, firmware and wearable CAD.

Four versions of the wearable CAD, left to right: finger IMU mounts, small-IMU mounts, micro-IMU (J2) mounts and the full gauntlet, each on a reference hand.

Hand tracking50 → 200 Hz
Exo accuracy0.5° per joint (Hall effect)
Validation16 mm median vs video
IMUs6/9-DOF mix, BNO085 + ISM330
LinksUSB · BLE · WiFi
  • IMU architecture chosen to keep electronics small on the back of the hand.
  • Data-collection glove with palm and fingertip tactile sensing working within a month.

07 · Side project · 2026

3D-printed planetary actuator

A two-stage planetary gearbox built around a hobby outrunner, designed to be printed and assembled on a desk. A separate design from the actuators used at Foundation.

Reduction22.6 : 1, two stages
Stage 15.14 : 1 · sun 14T, planets 22T, ring 58T, m1
Stage 24.40 : 1 · sun 15T, planets 18T, ring 51T, m1.25
EnvelopeØ110 × 106 mm
MotorOutrunner, up to 88 × 26 mm
Bearings2 × 6810-2RS output, 12 × 623ZZ planet
Parts21 printed · 15 bought
  • Both ring gears are cut into one printed housing, so the two stages share a single bore.
  • Sun 1 bolts straight to the motor's rotor: no shaft, no coupling.
  • Every planet runs on a pair of 623ZZ bearings instead of a printed bushing.

09 · UIUC Human Dynamics & Control Lab · 2025

Assistive torso-driven backrest

An instrumented backrest and seat that read a seated user's torso motion, used to drive a virtual omnidirectional robot hands-free through a VR course.

Torso angle< 2° error, encoder + ToF fusion
Signal> 90% consistency
MechanismPivot with torsion spring, hard angle limits
ComparedJoystick vs IMU vs seat + IMU vs seat + backrest
